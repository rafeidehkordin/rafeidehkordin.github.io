About

I came to AI engineering through research. My doctorate at Northeastern University spent five years applying machine learning to civil and environmental engineering problems, work that taught me to be skeptical of models that only perform well in the lab. That instinct has shaped everything since: I care about evaluation, monitoring, and the unglamorous infrastructure that decides whether a system survives contact with real users.

Today I am an AI/ML engineer at VHB, where I design and build enterprise AI on Microsoft Azure. My current focus is agentic AI systems, LLMOps, and LLM evaluation: multi-agent platforms with human-in-the-loop controls, memory layers for enterprise assistants, and the validation pipelines that make language models safe to put in front of production data. Before VHB, I built cyber risk scoring and MLOps platforms at Munich Re.

I still read papers for pleasure, and I still think the hardest problems in AI are the ones between the model and the organization around it.
